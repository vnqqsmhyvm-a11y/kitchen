Pork Chile Verde

Simply Recipes' classic chile verde: broiled tomatillos, roasted garlic, jalapeño, and cilantro blended into a bright green sauce, then pork shoulder browned in olive oil and simmered in it for 2–3 hours. Weekend stew; tortillas and brown rice alongside. Leftovers become burritos.

Ingredients

Salsa verde

  • 1 1/2 lb tomatillos, husked and rinsed
  • 5 garlic cloves, unpeeled
  • 1 jalapeño, seeds and ribs removed, chopped (source uses 2 — see Notes)
  • 1 can (4 oz) mild diced green chiles (stands in for the optional Anaheim/poblano)
  • 1 bunch cilantro, chopped

Stew

  • 3 1/2 lb pork shoulder (butt), trimmed, 1–2 inch cubes
  • Salt and pepper
  • 2–3 Tbsp olive oil
  • 2 yellow onions, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 Tbsp dried oregano (or 2 Tbsp fresh)
  • 2 1/2 cups chicken stock
  • Pinch ground cloves
  • To serve: flour or corn tortillas; brown rice (house rule — source suggests Spanish rice)

Method

  1. Halve the tomatillos and put them cut-side down on a foil-lined sheet with the unpeeled garlic. Broil 5–7 minutes until lightly blackened. Cool a bit.
  2. Blend the tomatillos (skins and all), the garlic squeezed from its skins, jalapeño, green chiles, and cilantro until finely chopped.
  3. Season the pork well with salt and pepper. Brown it in olive oil in batches in a large heavy pot over medium-high. Set aside.
  4. Pour off all but ~1 Tbsp fat. Cook the onions and chopped garlic ~5 minutes until limp.
  5. Return the pork. Add oregano, the salsa verde, enough stock to cover, the cloves, and a little salt and pepper.
  6. Bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered 2–3 hours until fork-tender. Adjust seasoning.
  7. Serve in bowls with warm tortillas and brown rice.

Notes

  • Simply Recipes' 3 1/2–4 lb shoulder serves 8 — kept at 3 1/2 lb: dinner for 6 plus burrito leftovers.
  • Kid heat: the source calls for 2 jalapeños. One seeded jalapeño + a can of mild green chiles keeps the green-chile flavor without the burn. Add the second jalapeño if it's an adults-heavy night.
  • Tomatillos are in the regular produce section at Walmart and most Aldis.
  • Weekend / make-ahead: 2–3 hours on the stove. Freezes well.
  • Not Slow Cooker Pork Carnitas: carnitas are dry-crisped shredded pork; this is a saucy green stew.
  • Pork counts toward the ≤2 red-meat cap. Mediterranean ✓ on 3 of 4 only when served with brown rice (olive oil, tomatillo/onion vegetables, brown rice); fatty pork is the miss. With flour tortillas instead, it's 2 of 4. moderate.
